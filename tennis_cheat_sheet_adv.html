Net height0.914 m at center, 1.07 m at posts

Zone memory: right half = Deuce court (served on at deuce / odd point totals), left half = Ad court. Placement terms: down the middle = T, tight to the sideline = Wide, straight at the returner = Body.

02Scoring & Rules

Point · Game · Set

LevelRule
Point0 (Love) → 15 → 30 → 40 → win the game
DeuceAt 40-40 you must win by 2: Advantage → next point wins
GameFirst to meet the above wins one game
SetFirst to 6 games leading by ≥2; at 6-6 play a tiebreak
TiebreakFirst to 7 points leading by ≥2; serve 1 point, then alternate every 2

Format notes

  • Best of 3: most events and women's Grand Slams. Best of 5: men's Grand Slams.
  • Super tiebreak (to 10): often replaces a deciding set in doubles.
  • Deciding-set tiebreak: all four Slams now use a 10-point tiebreak at 6-6 in the final set.
  • No-ad scoring: some leagues/doubles play a single deciding point at 40-40 — receiver chooses the side.
  • Serve rotation: switch server each game; change ends after odd-numbered games (1, 3, 5…).
  • Tiebreak serving: first server serves 1 point (from deuce side), then players alternate 2 serves each; change ends every 6 points.
  • Let: a serve that clips the net and still lands in is replayed — no fault, score unchanged.

03Grips

Defined by which of the handle's eight bevels the base knuckle of the index finger sits on.

GripBevelUse / Character
Continental2Serve, volley, overhead, slice, defense — the all-purpose grip
Eastern forehand3Flat power, quick to net, forgiving; suits attacking styles
Semi-Western4Modern forehand standard — heavy topspin with power
Western5Extreme topspin, loves high contact; tough on low balls
Eastern backhand / 2H top hand1Main one-hander grip; the non-dominant hand on a two-hander uses it
Two-handed backhand: the bottom (dominant) hand typically uses Continental, the top (non-dominant) hand uses an Eastern forehand — it's essentially "a forehand with your off hand."

04Forehand

  • Unit turn: rotate shoulders and hips together to prepare — not just the arm swinging back.
  • Swing path: low-to-high produces topspin; make contact out in front, to the side.
  • Stance: open stance aids recovery and power; closed stance favors penetrating down-the-line balls.
  • Kinetic chain: push off ground → rotate hips → rotate shoulders → swing arm → wrist lag & snap.
  • Follow-through: finish over the opposite shoulder (windshield-wiper motion adds spin).
Common faults: contact too late, all-arm swinging, lifting the head early. Track the ball to the strings before you commit to power.

05Backhand

Two-handed

  • Stable, easy to load topspin; the top hand drives the swing like an off-hand forehand.
  • Full rotation, weight moving forward, contact slightly farther forward than the forehand.

One-handed

  • Greater reach and contact flexibility, smooth transition to slice and net; harder on high and heavy balls.
  • Contact well in front, hitting arm extended, off hand back for balance.

Backhand slice

  • High-to-low with a slightly open face creates backspin; the ball stays low and floats — great for changing pace, defending, and setting up approaches.

06Serve

Always use the Continental grip; forearm pronation generates speed and spin. A consistent toss is the foundation of everything.

TypeSpinUse
FlatAlmost noneFirst-serve weapon, hunting aces; toss slightly more forward
SliceSidespinPulls the returner wide (especially from the deuce court); low bounce
Kick / TopspinTopspin + sideGo-to second serve: clears the net high, bounces high and away — safe and reliable

Placement combos (tied to tactics)

  • T: compresses the returner's angle, sets up your next-ball attack or approach.
  • Body: straight at the returner, forcing an awkward reply — very effective in doubles.
  • Wide: opens the court to create space for the following shot.
1st / 2nd serve strategy: first serve chases quality (placement + speed); the second serve prioritizes reliable spin and placement over just getting it in. Cutting double faults is what wins matches.

07Return of Serve

The return is the second-most-hit shot in tennis — getting it back in play already wins points against most amateurs. Split step as the server makes contact.

SituationPositionGoal
1st serveDeeper, near/behind baselineBlock / chip deep, neutralize — just start the rally
2nd serveStep inside the baselineTake time away, drive deep or attack, dictate
  • Block return: short, firm punch — like a volley off the bounce — for big first serves.
  • Chip & charge: slice the return low and follow it to net to pressure a weak second serve.
  • Read cues: watch toss placement and stance; on kick serves move up and take it before it climbs.
  • Targets: cross-court is the high-percentage default (longer court, lower net); down-the-line to surprise.
Doubles return: get it low — at the net player's feet or cross-court past them — to deny an easy poach, then move in.

08Volley & Overhead

Volley

  • Continental grip, block don't swing; contact in front of the body with a very short backswing.
  • Slightly open face for underspin and depth; step forward into the ball off your split step.
  • On low balls bend the knees, drop your level, open the face to carry the ball over.

Overhead / Smash

  • Turn sideways, point at the ball with the off hand to track it, then swing like a serve.
  • Use shuffle / crossover steps to get under the ball; aim deep cross-court or into open space.

Disguise is everything: the best drop shots and lobs look identical to a drive until the last instant. Same prep, change only at contact.

10Spin Mechanics

SpinSwingFlight / BounceTactical value
TopspinLow → highDips over net, high bounceForgiving, lets you hit big and force high contact
SliceHigh → lowLow, floating, skidsChange of pace, defense, approach setup, returns
FlatLevel / linearFast, flat trajectoryFinishing weapon, but low margin
Magnus effect: topspin creates a pressure difference above and below the ball, pulling it down faster — which is why you can hit hard and keep it in.

11Footwork & Movement

  • Split step: a small hop landing the instant your opponent strikes, loading to push off any direction.
  • Adjustment steps: small steps once you arrive, dialing in the ideal spacing to the ball.
  • Movement type: shuffle for short distances, crossover steps for covering ground fast.
  • Recovery: after hitting, move back along the bisector of your opponent's possible angles, not straight to the center mark.
  • Timing: taking the ball on the rise steals time; at the peak is most stable; on the drop you're defending.

12Singles Tactics

  • Percentage tennis: most points are lost to unforced errors. Build with consistent depth first, then look to attack.
  • Control the middle: deep balls pin the opponent behind the baseline and limit their angles.
  • Move & open angles: work them side to side, then finish cross-court or down the line into the gap.
  • Attack the weakness: most amateurs are weaker on the backhand — apply steady pressure and change direction.
  • Finish at net: use an approach shot to close in and cut down the opponent's reaction time.
  • Serve patterns: serve + forehand (serve+1) to attack, or serve-and-volley.
  • Returns: adjust position to serve quality; attack the second serve by stepping in.

13Doubles Tactics

Formations

  • One up, one back: common base setup; the serving team's net player looks to poach.
  • Both up: the attacking posture — control the net and seize the midcourt.
  • I-formation / Australian: net player straddles the center line to scramble the returner's targets.

Core play

  • Poach: the net player cuts laterally to intercept the return for a surprise put-away.
  • Return and approach: follow the return forward to contest the net.
  • Hit the middle / at the feet: low balls that force awkward replies between or below the opponents.
  • Communicate: use hand signals before each point to set poaching and who covers the middle.

Adjust your game: on slow courts add margin and grind; on fast/low courts bend your knees, shorten the backswing, and look to the net earlier.

15Equipment

Racket

SpecEffect
Head sizeLarger = more power & forgiveness; smaller = more control
Weight / balanceHeavier & head-light = stability + comfort; lighter = easier to swing
StiffnessStiffer = more power but more shock to the arm; flexible = more comfort/control
String patternOpen (16×19) = more spin/power; dense (18×20) = control & durability

Strings & tension

  • Polyester (poly): control + spin, stiff — popular but hardest on the arm.
  • Natural gut / multifilament: power, comfort, arm-friendly; gut is priciest.
  • Tension: higher = more control, less power; lower = more power & comfort, more spin. Lower tension and softer strings ease elbow strain.
Arm health: if you feel the elbow, the cheapest fixes are lower tension, a softer string (or thinner gauge), and a slightly more flexible frame — before changing technique.

16Mental & Match Play

  • Between-points routine: turn to the back fence, breathe, reset — a fixed ritual steadies nerves and tempo.
  • Play the big points simply: on break/set points go to your highest-percentage pattern, not your flashiest shot.
  • Score awareness: 30-0 / 40-15 are free points to take a risk; 30-30 and deuce are when discipline pays.
  • Control the controllables: effort, attitude, footwork, routine — not the call, the wind, or the last miss.
  • Momentum & tactics: when losing, change something — height, spin, pace, position — to break your opponent's rhythm.
  • Body language: stay positive between points; visible frustration hands the opponent confidence.
One point at a time: you can't win the match on any single point, but you can lose your composure on one. Win the next point.

17Conditioning & Training

DimensionFocus
Energy systemsMostly anaerobic intervals (short sprints + recovery) over an aerobic base
PowerBox jumps, medicine-ball throws, short sprints — improve first step and racket-head speed
AgilityLadder drills, suicides, change-of-direction work — sharpen split step and recovery
CoreAnti-rotation (planks, wood-chops) to transmit the kinetic chain
Mobility / preventionRotator cuff, hips, wrists, ankles; dynamic warm-up before, stretch after
RecoverySleep, hydration, nutrition, and load management are the real keys to staying injury-free
Common overuse injuries: tennis elbow (lateral), rotator cuff, knees and Achilles. A clean kinetic chain plus appropriate string tension protects the elbow more than "just hitting more."
